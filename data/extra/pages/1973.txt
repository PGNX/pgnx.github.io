Admission requirements
The master's degree course 'Data Science' can only be started in winter semester. Admission requirements are:
Basic knowledge of linear algebra, calculus, statistics, and programming
Bachelor’s degree or a comparable qualification
Proof of English language skills at B2 CEFR level or a comparable qualification*
German language skills at DSH 1 level (equivalent to B2 CEFR)*
Further information on admission and language proficiency requirements for foreign applicants is available on this page.
This master’s degree course is about…
The master’s degree course Data Science takes an interdisciplinary approach, recognising that data-driven solutions are applicable across a wide range of fields and industries. By combining core principles from statistics, computer science, mathematics, and domain-specific knowledge, this degree course equips students with the tools and expertise needed to tackle complex problems in various domains such as healthcare, finance, business, engineering, and social sciences.

Graduates of this degree course
Graduates are equipped with a diverse set of skills that prepare them for work in academia, at research institutions, or tech companies.
Some potential lines of employment are:
- Healthcare optimisation, disease prediction, drug development;
- Climate change studies, sustainable development, energy optimisation;
- AI in education
- Natural language processing, intelligent tutoring systems, or recommendation systems;
- Development of intelligent systems, urban planning, or agricultural optimisation;
- Computational biology and bioinformatics;
- Computational social science
General information about the degree course
The degree course is designed to educate experts not only in data science but also to integrate the knowledge from their undergraduate studies, enabling them to apply data science techniques effectively within their specific field of expertise.
What is special about studying Data Science in Greifswald?
The degree course provides students with the opportunity to realise projects in collaboration with leading local non-university research institutes, such as the Friedrich-Loeffler-Institut, the Max-Planck Institute (IPP), the Leibniz Institute for Plasma Research and Technology (INP), and the Helmholtz Institute for One Health. Students also benefit from access to specialised labs and high-performance computing resources, enhancing their research capabilities.
